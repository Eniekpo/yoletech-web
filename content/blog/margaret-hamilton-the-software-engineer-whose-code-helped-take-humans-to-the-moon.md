### Who Created It?

Margaret Hamilton led the software engineering team responsible for developing the onboard flight software used in NASA's Apollo command and lunar modules.

She worked alongside hundreds of engineers, programmers, and scientists. The Apollo software was a collaborative engineering achievement, not the work of one individual.

### When and Where Was It Developed?

Development took place during the 1960s at MIT's Instrumentation Laboratory in Cambridge, Massachusetts, under a contract with NASA.

Hamilton joined the Apollo software effort in 1965 and later became a leader of the software development team.

### How Was It Developed?

The team developed software for the Apollo Guidance Computer, which controlled important spacecraft operations.

The software handled:

* Guidance and navigation.
* Flight control.
* Astronaut interface systems.
* Error detection and recovery.
* Prioritization of critical computer tasks.

A particularly important feature was priority scheduling. When the computer became overloaded, it could suspend less important tasks and prioritize essential operations.

During Apollo 11's 1969 lunar landing, the computer generated alarms 1201 and 1202 because of processing overload. Its software prioritized essential landing operations, allowing the mission to continue.

### What Problem Did It Solve?

Spacecraft computers had limited processing capacity. They needed to handle several operations simultaneously while responding to unexpected conditions.

A computer failure during a critical flight operation could endanger the astronauts.

Hamilton's team developed software that could recognize problems, prioritize important operations, and recover from certain errors without immediately stopping essential mission functions.

### Why Is It Relevant Today?

Hamilton's contribution helped establish the importance of reliable software engineering.

The principles demonstrated during Apollo remain relevant in:

* Aviation and aerospace systems.
* Medical equipment.
* Automotive safety systems.
* Industrial automation.
* Artificial intelligence infrastructure.
* Mission-critical software development.

Hamilton later founded Higher Order Software in 1976 and Hamilton Technologies in 1986. She received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2016.

Her work demonstrated that software must be carefully engineered, tested, and designed to handle unexpected situations, especially when human safety depends on it.
