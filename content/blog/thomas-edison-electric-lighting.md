**Who Was Thomas Edison?**

Thomas Alva Edison (1847–1931) was an American inventor and businessman known for developing several influential technologies, including the phonograph, motion-picture equipment, and a practical incandescent electric lighting system.

Although Edison is popularly credited with inventing the light bulb, electric lighting had been studied by many inventors before him. His major contribution was developing a reliable, longer-lasting incandescent lamp and the electrical infrastructure needed to make electric lighting commercially useful.

### **When and Where Did He Develop It?**

Edison began intensive work on practical electric lighting in 1878 at his Menlo Park laboratory in New Jersey, United States.

On October 22–23, 1879, his research team successfully tested a carbonized cotton-thread filament that burned for approximately 14.5 hours.

On December 31, 1879, Edison publicly demonstrated his electric lighting system at Menlo Park.

He received U.S. Patent No. 223,898 on January 27, 1880.

### **How Was the Light Bulb Developed?**

Edison and his research team experimented with thousands of materials to find a suitable filament.

Their work focused on three important requirements:

1. **Filament:** Finding a material that could become hot enough to produce light without burning out quickly.
2. **Vacuum:** Removing air from the glass bulb to reduce filament deterioration.
3. **Electrical efficiency:** Developing a high-resistance filament that could operate effectively within a practical electrical distribution system.

The team eventually developed a carbon-filament incandescent lamp.

Edison also worked on generators, electrical wiring, switches, meters, and distribution systems. These components made it possible to supply electric lighting to buildings and businesses.

### **What Problem Did It Solve?**

Before practical electric lighting, many homes and businesses depended on candles, oil lamps, and gas lighting.

These alternatives had limitations, including fire hazards, smoke, maintenance requirements, and restricted lighting performance.

Earlier electric lamps also struggled with short operating lives and commercial practicality.

Edison's improvements helped make indoor electric lighting more reliable and suitable for widespread use.

### **Why Is His Invention Relevant Today?**

Edison's work helped establish the foundations of the modern electric lighting industry.

In September 1882, his Pearl Street Station in New York began supplying electricity commercially to customers.

His contributions helped accelerate the transition from gas lighting to electric power.

Modern lighting has since evolved through incandescent, fluorescent, compact fluorescent, and LED technologies.

Today, electric lighting is essential in:

* Homes and residential buildings.
* Hospitals and healthcare facilities.
* Schools and universities.
* Factories and industrial facilities.
* Offices and commercial buildings.
* Streets and public infrastructure.

**Historical clarification:** Edison did not invent the first electric light bulb. British inventor Joseph Swan independently developed a practical incandescent lamp in the late 1870s. Other scientists had also contributed to electric lighting before both men. Edison's distinctive achievement was developing and commercializing a practical lighting system in the United States.
